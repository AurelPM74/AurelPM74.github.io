abstract: "This paper studies quantum key-recovery attacks on block ciphers based on truncated differentials. It adapts a quantum convolution algorithm, previously used for linear cryptanalysis, to compute differential probabilities and prepare quantum states that support key search. The resulting method can achieve better-than-quadratic speedups in certain theoretical settings, although not yet for practical ciphers. The framework also covers related-key attacks and is applied to reduced-round QARMAv2-64 and AES-256. The analysis accounts for quantum memory requirements."
